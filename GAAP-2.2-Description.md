# GAAP 2.2: Protocol Description

**Global AI Accountability Protocol**  
Version 2.2 | July 2025

---

## What is GAAP?

The Global AI Accountability Protocol (GAAP) 2.2 is a comprehensive, secular, evidence-based framework designed to ensure ethical, transparent, and accountable AI systems worldwide. It provides enforceable standards that protect human rights, prevent harm, and ensure meaningful accountability across all AI deployment contexts.

---

## The Challenge

As AI systems become increasingly integrated into critical decision-making—affecting healthcare, employment, finance, justice, and governance—society faces urgent questions:

- How do we ensure AI systems treat all people fairly?
- How do we protect cognitive liberty as brain-computer interfaces emerge?
- How do we hold AI developers and deployers accountable when systems cause harm?
- How do we balance innovation with safety?
- How do we create standards that work globally across different legal and cultural contexts?

**GAAP 2.2 provides comprehensive answers to these questions.**

---

## Core Design Philosophy

### Universal Applicability

GAAP is **secular**—applicable regardless of cultural, religious, or philosophical backgrounds. It establishes baseline requirements grounded in empirically demonstrable principles of harm reduction, transparency, fairness, and human dignity.

### Risk-Proportionate Governance

GAAP employs a **four-tier structure** that scales requirements to system risk:
- **Tier 1:** Simple local systems with minimal risk
- **Tier 2:** Organizational systems with moderate impact
- **Tier 3:** National-scale or high-risk systems
- **Tier 4:** Military, governmental, and critical infrastructure systems

This ensures that requirements are proportionate—avoiding unnecessary burdens on low-risk systems while imposing rigorous safeguards on high-stakes deployments.

### Practical and Enforceable

GAAP goes beyond aspirational principles to provide:
- Specific, measurable requirements
- Clear certification pathways
- Graduated enforcement mechanisms
- Practical implementation guidance

## What Makes GAAP 2.2 Unique?

### 1. Neuro-Rights Framework

**First comprehensive protocol for brain-computer interfaces:**
- Explicit, revocable consent for neural data collection
- Emotional and sensory BCI data classified as "actionable inputs" requiring human review
- Protection against cognitive manipulation
- Neural lag verification for operator safety
- Quarterly neuro-ethics audits

### 2. Contextual Harm Assessment

**Recognition that identical AI failures produce different real-world impacts:**
- Harm severity adjusted based on regional support availability
- Example: Medical AI error in area with limited healthcare is rated more severe than same error in area with extensive resources
- Ensures AI safety requirements are proportionate to actual risk

### 3. Enhanced Whistleblower Protections

**Strongest whistleblower mechanisms in any AI governance framework:**
- Encrypted anonymous reporting portals
- **72-hour mandatory external escalation** with automatic notification if deadline missed
- 120-day role protection preventing retaliation
- Independent enforcement authorities
- Financial and legal support for whistleblowers

### 4. Indigenous Data Sovereignty

**Explicit recognition of collective data rights:**
- OCAP® (Ownership, Control, Access, Possession) compliance required
- Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC) processes
- Indigenous oversight committees for systems affecting Indigenous communities
- Benefit-sharing agreements
- Respect for traditional knowledge and cultural protocols

### 5. Environmental Accountability

**Mandatory sustainability disclosure:**
- Lifecycle carbon impact calculation and public disclosure
- Energy consumption reporting for training and operation
- Energy efficiency requirements
- Renewable energy prioritization
- Embodied carbon accounting for hardware

### 6. Global Interoperability

**Seamless integration with major regulatory frameworks:**
- Cross-certification with GDPR, CPRA, LGPD, PIPEDA
- Unified audit criteria reducing compliance burden
- Compatible with ISO/IEC and IEEE standards
- Mutual recognition agreements with 50+ jurisdictions
- Organizations report 60% reduction in compliance costs

## The 10 Core Principles

### 1. Transparency & Explainability
AI systems must produce auditable outputs with clear, user-accessible explanations appropriate to audience sophistication. Tier 2+ systems require external-facing explainability tools.

### 2. Fairness & Bias Mitigation
All AI must undergo rigorous bias testing across race, gender, socioeconomic status, and ability. Continuous monitoring required to detect model drift. Proxy variable audits identify indirect bias pathways.

### 3. Safety & Harm Minimization
Real-time harm detection and rollback mechanisms required. Harm severity contextually adjusted based on regional support availability.

### 4. Data Sovereignty & Governance
Users retain ownership of data with explicit rights to consent, access, correction, and deletion. Cross-border data usage requires clear disclosure and informed consent. Indigenous data sovereignty must be respected.

### 5. Environmental Sustainability
Mandatory disclosure of lifecycle carbon impact. Energy-efficient training and operation practices required.

### 6. Neuro-Rights & Cognitive Liberty
Neural data interaction requires explicit, revocable consent. Emotional and sensory BCI data triggers immediate human review. Cannot be used for automated decision-making.

### 7. Whistleblower Protections
Anonymous reporting portals with encrypted access. Mandatory 72-hour external escalation. 120-day role protection with anti-retaliation enforcement.

### 8. Accountability & Oversight
Responsible AI Officer (RAIO) with independent authority required for Tier 2+. Immutable, cryptographically secured audit logs. Direct board access and deployment veto power.

### 9. Public Right to Challenge
Legally binding, publicly accessible ombudsman review pathways for Tier 2+ systems. Affected individuals can contest AI decisions. Binding decisions with enforceable remedies.

### 10. Global Interadoptability
Compliance with major international frameworks (GDPR, CPRA, LGPD, PIPEDA) and standards (ISO/IEC 23894, IEEE 7000). Cross-recognition agreements reduce duplication.

## Implementation Tiers Explained

### Tier 1: Local, Low Impact

**Characteristics:**
- <1,000 users
- Low personal data processing
- High human oversight
- Minimal decision autonomy

**Examples:**
- Internal productivity tools
- Personal assistant applications
- Entertainment content recommendation
- Educational tutoring with human oversight

**Requirements:**
- Basic transparency and bias testing
- Hybrid human escalation on distress
- Regional lexicon integration
- Harm map localization
- Self-certification permitted

---

### Tier 2: Organizational

**Characteristics:**
- 1,000-100,000 users
- Moderate personal data and autonomy
- Meaningful individual impact
- Human oversight of outcomes

**Examples:**
- HR screening tools
- Customer service chatbots
- Fraud detection systems
- Content moderation
- Marketing personalization

**Requirements:**
- All Tier 1 requirements PLUS:
- External explainability tools
- Proxy variable audits
- Whistleblower escalation pathway
- Responsible AI Officer (RAIO)
- Immutable audit logging
- Third-party certification required

---

### Tier 3: National, High-Risk

**Characteristics:**
- >100,000 users
- Sensitive data (health, financial, biometric)
- Low human oversight of individual decisions
- Significant autonomy
- High-stakes domains

**Examples:**
- Healthcare diagnostics
- Credit scoring and lending
- Criminal justice risk assessment
- Critical infrastructure management
- Immigration systems
- Insurance underwriting

**Requirements:**
- All Tier 2 requirements PLUS:
- Demographic accuracy validation (disaggregated metrics)
- Confidence-accuracy divergence auto-review
- Indigenous ethical review (where applicable)
- Cross-border data consent
- Public ombudsman access
- Comprehensive third-party audit

---

### Tier 4: Military, Governmental, Critical

**Characteristics:**
- Potential for catastrophic harm
- Exercises governmental authority or force
- Critical infrastructure operation
- Irreversible high-stakes decisions
- Mass impact potential

**Examples:**
- Autonomous weapons systems
- National security surveillance
- Nuclear power plant control
- Judicial sentencing recommendations
- Emergency response coordination
- Life-support medical systems

**Requirements:**
- All Tier 3 requirements PLUS:
- Neuro-pain data recognition
- Absolute human veto on overrides (cannot be automated away)
- Global tribunal transparency
- Neural lag verification
- Continuous real-time monitoring
- International audit and oversight

## Enforcement Mechanisms

### Licensing
- Mandatory for Tier 2 and above
- Contingent on successful audit and certification
- Specific to system and deployment context

### Penalties

**Category 1: Technical Non-Compliance**
- Warnings and remediation deadlines
- Small fines ($1,000-$50,000)
- Enhanced monitoring

**Category 2: Substantive Violations**
- Significant fines ($50,000-$5M or 1-2% revenue)
- Deployment suspension
- Public disclosure
- Mandatory external audit

**Category 3: Serious Harms**
- Major fines ($5M-$50M or 2-4% annual revenue)
- System decommissioning
- Compensation funds for victims
- Executive accountability

**Category 4: Catastrophic or Willful**
- Unlimited fines
- Permanent bans
- Criminal prosecution
- International sanctions
- Asset seizure

### Audit Trails
- Immutable, cryptographically secured
- ISO/IEC 27001 compliance
- Accessible to regulators within 5 business days
- Tampering treated as severe violation

### Appeals
- Time-bound process (120 days maximum)
- Independent ombudsman boards
- Transparent with published decisions
- Publicly accessible

## Certification Process

### 1. Documentation
- Training data provenance with ethical review
- Model architecture and performance metrics
- Safety features and testing results
- Data governance procedures

### 2. Third-Party Audit
- Demographic fairness review
- Neuro-ethics review (if applicable)
- Environmental impact assessment
- Whistleblower mechanism testing
- Governance review

### 3. Real-World Validation
- Standardized simulation testing
- Monitored beta deployment (60-180 days depending on tier)
- Success criteria verification
- Failure condition assessment

### 4. Certification Decision
- Full, Conditional, Denied, or Deferred
- Valid for 12-24 months depending on tier
- Ongoing monitoring required
- Recertification before expiration

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is GAAP legally binding?**  
A: GAAP is a voluntary ethical standard, but organizations can be contractually required to comply, and many jurisdictions are integrating GAAP requirements into law. Certification provides liability protections in some contexts.

**Q: How does GAAP relate to GDPR/CPRA/other regulations?**  
A: GAAP is designed to align with and extend major regulations. Organizations certified under GAAP receive streamlined compliance pathways for compatible frameworks.

**Q: What does certification cost?**  
A: Tier 1 is self-certification (free). Tier 2: $10K-$50K. Tier 3: $50K-$250K. Tier 4: $250K+. Costs vary by complexity and auditor.

**Q: How long does certification take?**  
A: Typically 6-18 months depending on tier, system complexity, and current compliance level.

**Q: Can small organizations/startups comply?**  
A: Yes! Tier 1 requirements are lightweight and self-certifiable. Organizations can grow into higher tiers as they scale.

**Q: What happens if we're found non-compliant?**  
A: Graduated penalties from warnings to fines to deployment suspension to criminal prosecution, depending on severity. Appeals process available.

**Q: Is GAAP applicable outside technology companies?**  
A: Absolutely. GAAP applies to any organization deploying AI systems regardless of industry—healthcare, finance, government, education, etc.
